## What is the difference between parfum, EDP and EDT?

The difference is how much perfume oil (the fragrance concentrate) is dissolved in alcohol. More oil generally means a richer scent that lasts longer and needs fewer sprays.

The figures below are typical ranges. There is no single legal standard, and each house sets its own.

| Name | Typical concentration | What to expect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Parfum (also extrait de parfum) | About 20–30% | The richest; sits closer to the skin and lasts the longest |
| Eau de parfum (EDP) | About 15–20% | Full-bodied and long-lasting; the most common choice for an everyday signature |
| Eau de toilette (EDT) | About 5–15% | Lighter and brighter; fades sooner |
| Eau de cologne (EDC) | About 2–4% | Very light and fresh; a short-lived splash |

## Does a higher concentration always last longer?

Usually, but not always. The ingredients matter as much as the strength. Citrus and other fresh top notes evaporate quickly at any concentration, while woods, resins, musks and amber linger. A woody eau de toilette can outlast a citrus eau de parfum.

Your skin and the weather matter too. Fragrance tends to fade faster on dry skin, and heat makes it project more strongly but for a shorter time.

## Is parfum stronger than eau de parfum?

It is more concentrated, but that doesn’t always make it louder. Parfum often has less alcohol to carry it, so it can wear closer to the skin while lasting longer. Eau de parfum tends to project a little further in the first hours.

## Which one should I choose?

- **For an everyday signature**, eau de parfum is a good balance of presence and lasting power.
- **For warm days or the office**, a lighter eau de toilette, or fewer sprays of an eau de parfum, keeps things discreet.
- **For evenings and special occasions**, parfum or eau de parfum gives the most depth.
