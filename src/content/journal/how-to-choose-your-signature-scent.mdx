## Where do I start?

Start from what you already like. If you love the smell of fresh laundry, citrus or the sea, look at fresh and aquatic fragrances. If you are drawn to vanilla, spice or incense, look at amber, gourmand and woody ones. Your existing favourites, in perfume or in everyday life, are the best clue.

## What are the main scent families?

- **Fresh, citrus and aquatic:** bright, clean and airy.
- **Floral:** from soft petals to heady white flowers.
- **Woody:** sandalwood, cedar, vetiver and oud; warm and grounding.
- **Amber and spicy:** resins, vanilla and warm spices; rich and enveloping.
- **Gourmand:** edible notes such as vanilla, caramel and coffee.

Most perfumes combine several families, but one usually leads.

## How should I test a perfume?

Test it on your own skin, not only on a paper strip. Your skin changes how a fragrance smells. Try no more than two or three at a time, one on each wrist or arm, so your nose doesn’t tire.

## How long should I wait before deciding?

At least a few hours. A perfume changes as it dries. The first minutes are the top notes, which fade quickly. The heart appears after a while, and the base, which is what you will live with most of the day, comes through last. Decide on the dry-down, not the first spray.

## Should my signature scent change with the seasons?

It can. Many people keep one signature and wear it more lightly in the heat, or keep a fresher scent for summer and a richer one for cooler months. Read [Best perfumes for summer](/journal/best-perfumes-for-summer) for more.
