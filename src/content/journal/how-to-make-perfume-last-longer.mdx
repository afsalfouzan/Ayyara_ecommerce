## Where should I apply perfume so it lasts?

Apply it to pulse points: the inside of the wrists, the base of the throat, behind the ears and the inside of the elbows. These spots are warm, and warmth helps the fragrance unfold over the day.

## Should I moisturise first?

Yes. Fragrance holds better on moisturised skin than on dry skin. Apply an unscented lotion first, so it doesn’t compete with the perfume, then spray straight after.

## Is it bad to rub perfume into my wrists?

Rubbing doesn’t ruin a perfume, but it warms the skin and speeds up how quickly the lighter top notes evaporate, so the opening fades sooner. Spray and let it dry on its own.

## When is the best time to apply perfume?

Just after a shower, once your skin is dry. Clean skin carries fragrance evenly, and slightly warm skin helps it develop.

## Can I spray perfume on my clothes or hair?

Fabric often holds scent longer than skin, but perfume can mark delicate or light-coloured fabrics, so test an inside seam first. For hair, spray a little onto a brush rather than directly, as the alcohol can be drying.

## How should I store perfume?

Keep it somewhere cool, dark and dry, with the cap on. Heat, sunlight and humidity break down fragrance over time, so a bathroom shelf or a sunny windowsill is the worst place for it. A drawer or the original box is ideal.

## Does the type of perfume matter?

Yes. An eau de parfum generally lasts longer than an eau de toilette, and scents built on woods, amber and musk outlast fresh, citrus-led ones. Read more in [EDP vs EDT vs Parfum](/journal/edp-vs-edt-vs-parfum).
